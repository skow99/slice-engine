# Sailing physics and harbour scale for Mazury

Research for the next Lake Niegocin prototype, 24 November 2026.

## Give the boat, lake and berths the same scale

The current terrain is **3 m per map pixel**. The sailboat stack is 33 pixels long, so its nominal footprint is **99 m**. The 16-pixel contact radius represents **48 m**, the 38-pixel mooring radius represents **114 m**, and the 22-pixel-per-second drive setting represents **66 m/s**, or about **128 knots**. The old 8-pixel-per-second mooring gate also represents about **47 knots**. These values were tuned for the short demo. Together they explain why the lake feels small and why port-scale manoeuvring cannot work at the current scale. [Map resolution](../map/README.md) · [Boat art and dimensions](../main.js) · [Hull and speed settings](../sailing.js) · [Mooring radius](../lake.js)

Use a generic Mazurian cabin sloop with the **Antila 24.4** as the dimensional reference. The builder lists a 7.35 m hull, 8.20 m overall length, 2.70 m beam, 29.5 m² total sail area and 0.32/1.36 m draft with its swing centreboard raised/lowered. These dimensions establish scale. For force calibration, obtain separate sail dimensions, loaded displacement, stability data and a speed trial or polar for the chosen setup. The builder's published 2,200 kg figure is transport mass. [Antila Yachts](https://www.antila-yachts.pl/) · [Giżycko charter fleet specification](https://jachtymazury.pl/antila-24-4/)

- Store positions, hull footprints, berth shapes and velocities in metres and seconds. Convert the survey grid at the map boundary and project the same geometry at each zoom level. At the current survey resolution, a 7.35 m hull spans only **2.45 pixels**. As a starting camera setting, **0.25 m per game pixel** puts it at about **29 pixels** and shows about **128 m** across the 512-pixel view. Use a wider lake chart and a close harbour view. Keep the collision hull independent of sprite resolution.
- Use the current Niegocin shoreline vectors for larger-scale geometry. Build harbour entrance, breakwater, fairway, pier and berth outlines from adequately detailed plans or georeferenced imagery. A pier finger between 2 and 3 m wide can disappear in a 3 m raster. Use oriented hull collision against the detailed geometry and sweep it between simulation steps so the boat cannot pass through a thin dock.
- The current Giżycko–Rydzewo chart polyline is **8.18 km**. At an illustrative 4–6 knots it would take **44–66 minutes** before manoeuvring or tacking. Keep the physics clock at real scale for harbour work and manoeuvre practice. Short objectives leave a three-minute session possible. A later transit mode could explicitly advance simulation time while retaining the same boatspeed, apparent wind and interaction scale. ## Model motion and loads over time

Use four degrees of freedom for the first playable model. Integrate forward/backward motion, sideways motion, heading and heel, plus their rates. A velocity prediction program supplies a reference for steady speed and equilibrium heel across different winds and sail plans. A time-domain model supplies the momentum and transient forces needed for turns, gusts and docking. Masuyama and colleagues compared modelled tacks against differential GPS tracks from full-scale cruiser trials. Their results support the modelling approach, while the coefficients belong to their test yacht. [ORC VPP 2026, chapters 3–5](https://orc.org/uploads/files/Rules-Regulations/2026/ORC-VPP-Documentation-2026.pdf) · [Full-scale manoeuvring and tacking experiments](https://doi.org/10.2534/jjasnaoe1968.1993.174_377) · [Fossen marine craft dynamics](https://www.fossen.biz/html/marineCraftModel.html)

1. Sample the local true wind as an air velocity over ground. With vectors pointing in the direction of motion, compute **apparent airflow = true airflow − boat velocity over ground**. Airflow at a sail can later include the motion of its centre of effort as the boat yaws or rolls. Use **boat velocity − water velocity** for the underwater forces. Display wind direction using the “from” convention. [NOAA relative wind conversions, appendix A7](https://downloads.psl.noaa.gov/psd3/cruises/VOCALS_2008/RHB/Fairall-Revised-TM-4-23-07.pdf) · [Dynamic sailboat model, sections 2.1–2.2](https://tuprints.ulb.tu-darmstadt.de/8471/7/sailboat_model_irsc.pdf)
2. Give **grot** and **fok** separate sail areas, centres of effort, deployment states and sheet limits. For each sail, use the angle between the apparent airflow and the sail chord to calculate lift and drag. A practical starting form is `q = ½ρV²`, lift `q × area × C_L` perpendicular to the flow and drag `q × area × C_D` along it. Use smooth, signed coefficients for attached flow, stall, and large downwind angles. Give a loosely sheeted, luffing sail little useful drive. A sheeted sail can still exert force after flow separates. Allow the main to partly shelter the jib on a run. Use generic coefficients as tuning inputs until a matching two-sail rig can be measured. [ORC VPP 2026, chapter 5](https://orc.org/uploads/files/Rules-Regulations/2026/ORC-VPP-Documentation-2026.pdf) · [Full-scale sail force measurements](https://doi.org/10.2534/jjasnaoe1968.1997.1)
3. Let the hull slow through drag, and let the centreboard oppose sideways motion through hydrodynamic force as it gains flow. Some **leeway** remains. Left/right changes the blade angle at a limited rate. Rudder force and yaw authority grow with water flowing past the blade, so a stopped hull under sail cannot pivot on command. Use the local direction of flow so sternway and forward motion feel distinct. Apply the forces at their working points to produce yaw. A heeled boat may develop weather helm and tend to round into the wind. Tune that coupling to be gentle at normal heel and stronger when overpowered. [Dynamic sailboat model, sections 2.2–2.3](https://tuprints.ulb.tu-darmstadt.de/8471/7/sailboat_model_irsc.pdf) · [UK Sailmakers on weather helm](https://www.uksailmakers.com/encyclopedia/glossary/weather-helm/)
4. Compute **heel** from sideways sail load and its lever arm. Continue integrating roll as the hull and ballast push it upright and water damps the motion. A spring and damper are a useful approximation near upright. Increase fidelity with a measured righting-moment curve if one becomes available. Render the hull and rig leaning together about the waterline, with small wave-driven bob and pitch. Slice Engine has a low-level stack shear that can approximate heel using a bounded number of poses. Sail movement still needs separate rig geometry with correctly placed mast, boom and forestay pivots. [ORC VPP 2026, sections 3.3.2 and 4.4](https://orc.org/uploads/files/Rules-Regulations/2026/ORC-VPP-Documentation-2026.pdf) · [Slice Engine pose renderer](../src/voxel.js)

## Make the rig and manoeuvres visible

Both sails should have independent **postaw/zrzuć** controls, visible raising/lowering transitions, and changing drive and heel while they move. A furled or lowered sail contributes essentially no working area. A sail that is up can fill, luff or backwind depending on the wind and sheets. Use the sheets to limit how far the boom and clew can move. Let wind and sheet settings move the cloth towards its loaded pose over time. A relaxing default can assist with the sheets while leaving steering, sail choice and engine with the player. Provide trim controls wherever the coach suggests using them. [US Sailing Basic Keelboat standard](https://www.ussailing.org/education/adult/certification-courses-endorsements/basic-keelboat/) · [North Sails on steering, luffing and heel](https://www.northsails.com/en-us/blogs/north-sails-blog/steering-upwind)

| Manoeuvre | What the player should see and feel |
| --- | --- |
| **Zwrot przez sztag** · tack | The bow turns through the wind from close-hauled on one tack to close-hauled on the other. Both sails unload or luff as the boat crosses the no-go sector. It carries momentum, loses speed and settles on the new tack as the sheets and sails change sides. If it enters too slowly, it can stall head-to-wind. |
| **Zwrot przez rufę** · gybe | The stern passes through the wind from one broad reach to the other. The boom crosses, the jib changes sides and the boat rolls towards the new balance. Bring the boom under control for an assisted gybe. An unexpected airflow shift across the stern can also trigger an unplanned gybe. |

Both manoeuvres should be possible through normal steering. Optional buttons can assist with the helm and sheets by sending the same bounded controls, using the current wind and checking available speed and water room. Smooth or debounce the detection near the wind axis so minor fluctuations do not switch the sail rapidly. The RYA's approximate 40° upwind limit is an initial teaching reference. Derive the actual usable sector from the simulated yacht's speed and ability to make progress to windward. [PZŻ manoeuvre standards](https://pya.org.pl/polski-zwiazek-zeglarski/page/system-szkolenia-pzz/) · [World Sailing training, section four](https://www.sailing.org/tools/documents/ISAFLearnToSailProgrammeA4-%5B6277%5D.pdf) · [RYA points of sail](https://www.rya.org.uk/training/do-you-know-your-points-of-sail/)

On a run, the mainsail can blank the jib when both lie on the same side. Sailing **na motyla** exposes the jib on the opposite side while the main remains eased. The prototype uses true and apparent wind to decide when the rig can hold that configuration, and retains the current side of the main on entry. The angle limits and drive gains are gameplay tuning. [North Sails downwind trim](https://www.northsails.com/en-uk/blogs/north-sails-blog/downwind-sail-trim-how-to-north-sails)


## Gusts, wave cues and steering advice

Use a shared wind field for boat forces, sail animation and water. Start with a prevailing wind and seeded patches that move downwind and build and fade over several seconds. A patch can alter both speed and direction. Within the same patch, change ripple density and contrast, animate telltales and load the rig. Higher apparent wind increases load roughly with speed squared at the same trim. A 10-to-15-knot apparent-wind change would multiply the dynamic pressure by **2.25**. Heel should build and settle with inertia. Show short ripples and patches of darker or brighter surface before larger undulations develop, using a consistent lighting direction. A lake's shelter and exposed distance upwind affect how much chop builds. These are visual and physical modelling choices for the game, based on how wind affects surface roughness and wave growth. [ORC aerodynamic model](https://orc.org/uploads/files/Rules-Regulations/2026/ORC-VPP-Documentation-2026.pdf) · [NOAA surface roughness research](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/18847/noaa_18847_DS1.pdf) · [RYA weather, wind and waves](https://www.rya.org.uk/water-safety/weather-and-tides/weather-wind-and-waves/) · [MIT Sailing on reading wind on the water](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/pe-810-sailing-spring-2007/f7fdc138336e2a0437c598c7f789d6d8_booklet.pdf)

Show three directions with distinct indicators. The **heading** is where the bow points, **course over ground** is where the boat is actually moving, and the **target bearing** is where the selected waypoint or next harbour mark lies. Draw a target on the water, an edge cue when it leaves the screen, and a short bow-heading line. Let the player select a point or port on the chart. Hide unstable track readings at very low speed. [WMO/IOC ship navigation and wind definitions](https://icoads.noaa.gov/jcomm_tr13.pdf)

Coach from the same local wind and boat state. Label the point of sail in Polish, including **kąt martwy**, **bajdewind**, **półwiatr**, **baksztag** and **fordewind**. When the waypoint is upwind, show the reachable headings on either side and compare expected progress towards the target after leeway. For a safe route in the game, candidate headings must also clear islands and port structures. Give one actionable cue at a time, such as *Odpadnij, żeby napełnić żagle*, *Zbuduj prędkość przed sztagiem*, or *Zwrot przez rufę. Przygotuj grota*. Use a delay or angular margin to avoid flickering instructions in gusts. [PZŻ Digisail training manual](https://pya.org.pl/files/eSailing/Digisail-podrecznik-filmyPL.pdf) · [ORC polar and velocity-made-good method](https://orc.org/organization/rules-regulations)

## Motor and moor at real ports

Give the outboard an on/off state and independent **ahead, neutral and astern** thrust. Apply thrust at the stern. A steerable outboard can direct thrust at very low boatspeed. In neutral or with the motor stopped, the boat coasts and the water rudder responds to its actual flow. Windage can still push the hull sideways with both sails down. For mooring, add approach marks, a berth footprint and a berth heading. Check that the boat fits, is aligned where applicable, and has low forward and lateral speed. Only then offer **Cumuj**. Use a short line-settling motion and fender contact close to the berth, with an explicit cast-off action. The current repeat cast-off starts at rest and points only about 31.5° off the default wind. With a realistic no-go zone and rudder, that exit risks leaving the player in irons. Test powered departures and allow a push-off or suitable departure course for casting off under sail. ## Verify before expanding the simulation

1. Prove scale at one Ekomarina berth. A correctly sized hull clears the entrance, can motor in at low speed, can back out, and can make fast within the berth footprint.
2. Run repeatable open-water cases at fixed wind. Compare mirrored tacks and steady speeds at multiple points of sail. Check head-to-wind coast and stall, a tack that succeeds with way and one that fails without it, and a controlled and an unplanned gybe. The current northwesterly outward itinerary mostly reaches or runs, so use an explicit upwind trial as well.
3. Compare main-only, jib-only, both, and both down at the same wind and trim. Verify visible cloth and force changes and correct heel direction. Run one seeded gust and check that the ripple patch and boat response coincide.
4. Verify target guidance upwind and around an island, then repeat the berth checks at Wilkasy and Rydzewo. Record measured or playtested speeds, turn and coast distances, and heel response before treating any tuning values as representative of this yacht.

## Carrying way and timing thunder

A displacement hull carries way after propulsion drops and slows as water resists its motion. A keelboat’s rudder redirects water passing over its blade, so the helm still has authority while the boat is coasting and loses authority as that flow falls. The ORC yacht model makes viscous resistance depend on boat speed through both a squared-speed term and a Reynolds-number-dependent friction coefficient. It also accounts for wave-making resistance. [U.S. Coast Guard Boat Crew Handbook, section B.20](https://www.uscgaux-ocnj.org/Training/Crew%20Manuals/BOAT%20CREW%20HANDBOOK%20-%2016114.4A_Seamanship%20Fundamentals.pdf) · [US Sailing Basic Keelboat Instructor Manual, steering with a tiller](https://content.yudu.com/web/2vwiu/0A2vwiv/BKIM/html/48.html) · [ORC VPP 2026, chapter 6](https://orc.org/uploads/files/Rules-Regulations/2026/ORC-VPP-Documentation-2026.pdf)

In Mazury’s deterministic gameplay trials, the yacht turned 180° while coasting from 4.45 m/s in 6.85 s and finished at 1.83 m/s without using the bow jets. The engine-only trial started at 2.80 m/s, turned 180° in 8.35 s and finished at 1.00 m/s. These are outputs of the prototype’s tuned motion model. [Sailing simulation](../sailing.js)

The flash is visible almost immediately, while thunder takes about three seconds per kilometre to arrive. The first sound can come from the nearest part of the lightning channel. Mazury treats the strike as a point and divides its distance in metres by 343 m/s, the approximate sound speed in dry air at 20 °C. That gives a delay of about 2.9 seconds at 1 km.
