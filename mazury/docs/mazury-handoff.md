Milestone 1 is playable locally at `/mazury/`. From the start screen, select **Start approach** for the berth or **Sail out** for the shortened lake. At Ekomarina, follow the dashed approach, align with the marked berth, select neutral, and moor after the yacht slows. Select **Step ashore** to walk on the pontoon and waterfront. Return to the berth to board, then cast off again.

- **Controls** or **H** opens the complete keyboard reference. **←/→** steers, **↑/↓** shifts, **1/2/3** selects astern, neutral or ahead, **R** toggles the engine, **E** moors, **F** steps ashore or boards, and **G** sails out from the start screen.
- **B** or **Test boost** starts the engine in ahead and raises the target speed to about 33 knots for testing. Press it again to slow to normal ahead. Neutral, astern, engine off, and dropping anchor cancel boost.
- Normal sail and engine target speeds are double the earlier tuning. Acceleration, coasting and steering were retuned for the faster travel. The wind model uses the underlying sail speed for trim and heel.
- Steering automatically pulses a bow jet when there is too little water flow for the rudder. It works with the main engine off. Each pulse splashes on the opposite side of the turn and stops as the boat gathers way, the helm is released, or the boat is anchored or moored.
- Each steering pulse has a synthesized motor whine and water rush, panned toward the active jet. The sound follows the same thrust signal as the splash and uses the existing sound toggle. [Jet Thruster Micro rescue-sloop demo](https://www.jetthruster.com/new-jet-thruster-micro)
- The game plays ebunny's 68-second “Spanish Ladies” acoustic loop alongside the lake sounds after the player starts sailing. **Music** controls the soundtrack and remembers its setting. **Sound** mutes the entire mix.
- The propeller throws droplets and foam from the stern under power in ahead or astern. Test boost increases the spray, and the foam fades when thrust stops.
- **L** or the header clock opens the sky compass and time slider. **[ / ]** steps the clock by an hour and holds the selected time. **P** resumes or pauses it, and **N** toggles moonlight. At 1×, the clock advances one in-game minute per second during play. The sun follows a representative midsummer path for Niegocin. The moon uses a stylized full-moon path. [NOAA solar position equations](https://gml.noaa.gov/grad/solcalc/solareqns.PDF)
- Use **Game speed** in the header or **X** to select ½×, 1×, 2×, or 4×. It scales sailing, walking, wildlife and the running clock by applying more or fewer fixed simulation ticks.
- Click the wind readout or press **V** to set the direction the wind comes from, the steady speed from 0–28 kt, and the gust increase from 0–14 kt. **J** or **Gust now** sends a moving gust from upwind. Automatic gusts can be switched off separately. The same wind field drives the sail, roughens the water, and changes the breeze audio.
- Swan flocks are placed across the lake at startup. They swim into and out of view and sometimes approach a slow or anchored boat. A lone mallard flies in, lands on water, swims and can approach before taking off. Cormorants make periodic flights. All three use painted slice sheets and the prevailing scene lighting. [Bird art workflow and prompts](../art/README.md#water-birds)
- Crows turn on perches sampled from individual tree boughs. Their feet and landing targets follow the bough as it sways. Fish occasionally break the surface in open water, leaving two fading rings. [Crow art workflow and prompt](../art/README.md#crow)
- As the sun lowers, swans and the mallard head for the shoreline shallows, cormorants land on the bank, and crows settle on their perches. Routine flights and boat visits pause until sunrise. Skipping ahead to night lets birds finish their trips to shore, and the moonlight toggle leaves their schedule unchanged.
- The hull measures 7.35 × 2.70 m and the follow camera starts at 0.25 game metres per pixel. Ekomarina is one-to-one within a 320 m radius. `lake-world.js` shortens radial travel outside the harbour, with two transition bands and an outer ratio of 18:1. The chart displays the survey and uses the inverse projection to place the boat.
- The painted yacht now has 93 voxels along its hull, with cabin windows, a recessed teak cockpit, cleats, and finer rigging. The hull and both sails use detailed slices up close and a coarser bake from the same geometry at wide zoom. The display canvas increases its backing resolution on larger and high-DPI screens. [Yacht art workflow and prompts](../art/README.md#detailed-sailboat-and-jib)
- **Space** raises or lowers the mainsail (grot), and **Q/T** sheet it in or ease it. **U** raises or lowers the jib (fok), and **I/O** sheet it in or ease it. Both sails move through seven hoist stages. Lower both before mooring.
- The jib swings across the forestay as the apparent wind changes. It luffs near head-to-wind, adds drive when trimmed on a reach, and loses drive in the mainsail's wind shadow on a run. With both sails up and wind astern, **Y** selects **Na motyla**. The sheets ease and the jib opens opposite the main. The mode disengages when you steer away from the run or lower a sail. [RYA points of sail](https://www.rya.org.uk/training/do-you-know-your-points-of-sail/) · [North Sails downwind trim](https://www.northsails.com/en-uk/blogs/north-sails-blog/downwind-sail-trim-how-to-north-sails) The speed gain uses tuned prototype coefficients. The HUD can suggest separate sheet adjustments. [ORC VPP aerodynamic forces](https://orc.org/uploads/files/Rules-Regulations/2026/ORC-VPP-Documentation-2026.pdf)
- The boat heels away from the apparent wind in response to sail trim and hoist. Gusts increase heel and forward drive and push a loaded yacht sideways. The helm loses authority during a knockdown. Ease or lower sails to recover before the boat rolls over. **Enter** or **Right the boat** recovers from a capsize in place with both sails down. The thresholds are tuned for the prototype. The submerged centerboard becomes visible from 60° of heel and uses the same rolled geometry in the reflection. [ORC VPP roll moment equilibrium, section 3.3.2](https://orc.org/uploads/files/Rules-Regulations/2026/ORC-VPP-Documentation-2026.pdf#page=17)
- **+/−**, the mouse wheel, and the zoom buttons ease between camera levels around the yacht or the walker. The follow view interpolates between fixed simulation ticks, and the terrain scrolls at fractional pixel positions. **Z** pulls back to the live whole-lake view and returns to the previous follow zoom. The camera switches immediately when the browser requests reduced motion. **K** drops or weighs the anchor. A splash and a visible line from the bow show where the anchor went down. The anchor uses a fixed 3 m swing radius for the prototype without lakebed data.
- Ground relief uses the GUGiK height map. The close views and **Z** render lifted terrain slices, with trees rooted at their sampled elevations. **C** shows the surveyed chart.
- Close zooms use finer ground tiles for the waterline, gravel, reeds, meadow flowers and field rows. The sailing view places cottage and boathouse stacks at a spaced subset of surveyed building sites. At close zoom, their painted stacks show tiled roofs, masonry, windows, timber doors and chimneys. - Oak, silver birch, weeping willow, Scots pine, blue spruce and wild cherry use painted 192-layer sheets up close and 64-layer sheets in wider views. The close trees use roughly 8 cm voxels at nominal size, matching the yacht. Each species has three rotated and scaled variants at the original planting locations. Boughs sample the lake wind at the root and bend with a damped response. Birch and willow sprays trail their supporting branches. Water reflections share the canopy pose and shadows lean with the wind. [Lakeside tree art workflow](../art/README.md#lakeside-trees)
- Local terraces blend stone foundations and yards into the height-map slopes. Gravel footpaths connect to nearby mapped roads where a dry route fits. Ekomarina has a small gravel apron, a restaurant terrace and a harbormaster’s office. Close terrain detail distinguishes grass, sand, gravel, crops, reeds and scrub.
- The yacht's reflection mirrors its current hull and sail geometry across the waterline before projecting it. It tracks heading, heel, and sail trim. Tree and building reflections share the water mask, which clips projected land and quays. The local wind and gusts break up the reflections.
- `harbor.js` holds the orthophoto shoreline and a compact game marina with a walkable quay, three roughly 23–26 m pontoons and one marked 12 × 4.5 m berth. `harbor-view.js` draws layered dock structures and reuses the detailed cottage and boathouse stacks for Bosmanat and Restauracja. `sailing.js` handles propulsion, the hull, collisions and mooring.

## Crew ballast and automatic weather

- **Ballast** moves 0–240 kg of crew weight to the left or right side of the yacht. **Off** centers the crew and keeps the chosen weight. **4 / 5 / 6** select left, off or right. The heel gauge reports the current lean. Weight takes time to shift, works while stopped or moored, and can counter sail heel or add to it. The initial setting is off with 80 kg ready to shift.
- **Wind settings → Automatic weather** runs a procedural weather day from the lake clock. The typical afternoon breeze builds from lighter morning winds. Fronts change bearing, strength, gusts and rain together. Some days bring a faster-moving thunderstorm, and the next in-game day gets a new pattern. Wind, waves, warning towers, thunder and fleet decisions use the same conditions. Turning automation off restores the manual wind and rain settings. It starts off.
